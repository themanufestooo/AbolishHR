# Scoring Model

Every opportunity gets a score from 0–100 before you invest time in it. The score exists to prevent confusing activity with progress: a few strong applications beat dozens of weak ones.

## Score thresholds

| Score | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 85–100 | Exceptional | Apply — top priority |
| 75–84 | Strong | Apply |
| 65–74 | Conditional | Apply only if something specific makes it worthwhile (you decide) |
| Below 65 | Skip | Normally do not pursue |

## What the score combines

- **Compensation and upside** — pay vs. your floor; growth potential
- **Flexibility** — schedule fit, remote/hybrid options, time needs
- **Verified experience fit** — how well the role matches what you've actually done
- **Interview probability** — realistic chance of getting a response, given your background
- **Skill building** — whether the role grows you toward your goals
- **Commute / location** — distance and travel vs. your limits

**Red flags reduce the score.** Examples: pay far below your floor, schedule conflicts with hard constraints, requirements you don't meet, signs the listing is fake or a scam.

## Make it yours

These weights are a starting point, not a rule. A parent who needs predictable hours, a first-time worker, and someone protecting time for a business should not get the same ranking. Tell your assistant which factors matter most to you and it will adjust.

**One override always allowed:** if a role has personal meaning to you, you can pursue it regardless of score. The model advises; you decide.
