# Outreach Guide

Strong applications still need follow-up. A short, specific message to the right person often matters more than a tenth application.

## Finding the right people

For each high-fit role (score 75+), identify 1–3 relevant people:
- The hiring manager (often named in the posting, or findable by title)
- A recruiter at the company
- Someone on the team you'd join

Use the company's site and professional networks. Never guess email addresses — use listed contact info or platform messaging.

## Writing the message

Keep it short (under 150 words) and specific:

1. **Who you are, in one line:** "[NAME], [CURRENT/MOST RECENT ROLE] with [X] years in [FIELD]."
2. **Why them, specifically:** reference something real — their team, a project, the role itself. Generic flattery gets ignored.
3. **The ask:** one clear, low-pressure request — "Would you be open to a 15-minute conversation about the [ROLE] opening?"

**Never:** mass-message, copy-paste the same text to dozens of people, misrepresent your background, or follow up more than twice without a reply.

## Rules

- **Your identity, your send.** Outreach goes from your accounts, under your name. The assistant drafts; you send (or explicitly approve sending).
- **Rate limits:** a few thoughtful messages per day, not dozens. If a platform throttles you, stop — that's the platform telling you to slow down.
- **No spam.** One tailored message beats fifty generic ones, and mass outreach can get your accounts restricted.
- **Track everything:** who you contacted, when, what you said, and any reply — in the application tracker.
