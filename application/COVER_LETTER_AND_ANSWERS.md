# Cover Letters and Application Answers

## Cover letters

Not every application needs one — only write one when the posting asks for it or the role is high-fit (score 75+). Keep it short: three paragraphs.

1. **Why this role, specifically.** Name something real about the company or role — not flattery, a concrete connection to your experience.
2. **Your two strongest matching qualifications.** Truthful, drawn from your verified profile, tied to what the posting asks for.
3. **Logistics and close.** Availability, location fit, and a simple ask for a conversation.

Tone: plain and direct. No buzzwords, no desperation, no fiction.

## Short answers and screening questions

Many applications include open-ended questions ("Why do you want to work here?", "Describe a challenge you overcame").

- Answer from verified experience only.
- Keep answers to 3–5 sentences.
- If the assistant's confidence in an answer is low, it must pause and let you write or approve it — never guess on a required question.

## Salary questions

- Never commit to a number below your floor (see your opportunity policy).
- Preferred response when a form requires an answer: state your floor as a range, e.g. "Seeking [FLOOR]–[TARGET] per [HOUR/YEAR], open to discussion based on the full package."
- Any salary commitment outside your configured range requires your explicit approval first.
- In conversations, it's fine to say: "I'd like to understand the full role before discussing numbers."
